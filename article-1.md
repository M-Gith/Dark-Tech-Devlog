### What is our project?

What became more clear during this debrief meeting is what the project is actively about. The project is about developing a provocative prototype involving a smart mirror that recommends clothing based on the user’s appearance. The concept uses the mere exposure effect: the psychological phenomenon whereby repeated exposure to something can increase a person's familiarity with and preference for it. The goal with the mirror, is to demonstrate how recommendations can influence people’s preferences and purchasing decisions, while encouraging them to think critically of their own choices.

### Our deliverables

This project has three main deliverables:

1. **Interactive Experience:** A working prototype that demonstrates the mere exposure effect and raises awareness of this form of manipulation.
2. **Advisory Report:** An explanation of the prototype, how the system works, its ethical implications and possible more transparent alternatives.
3. **Test results:** Feedback and evidence testing with users, which can be used to understand the target audience and understand the prototype.

To be clear, the target audience consists of consumers who have bought fast-fashion products or are considering doing so. Other stakeholders include the project team, lecturers, the client, users participating in tests, fashion retailers, social media platforms, influencers, and clothing companies.

In this manner, we were able to design the smart mirror within slightly different contexts. This is so we can get more clarity of where we are headed to by showing multiple designs to the client. Which allows us to enter the next developmental cycle in a more ready state.

The subgroups and the smart mirror context:

We all started off by making a low fidelity design within Figma which we then showed to each other.

### Low Fidelity design

This part was made by my sub-group team member Ikrame. This low fidelity design gave us both and the team some expectations of where the mirror may be headed.

After this, we got in the design phase of making the physical prototype and the high fidelity design. Ikrame chose to make a physical prototype and I opted to make the high fidelity design and build upon the low fidelity design.

### Physical prototype

The physical prototype explores the experience of the smart mirror within a fitting room in a store. It allows us to get a feeling outside of the screen context and to already prime ourselves with the development of the physical aspect of the mirror. Through the physical design process, thoughts of how things may fit together when iterating towards the final concept may become more clear as opposed to only having Figma designs of the mirror.

### High Fidelity Design

Using aspects of the low fidelity design and the physical prototype. I was able to create a high fidelity design within Figma. This design builds further on the potential usecases of the smart mirror and simultaneously provides a more real world design.

For this high fidelity design I have chosen to use the fashion retailer H&M as an example for the styling of the design and where/how it could be used within the context of H&M. For some inspiration, I looked at the website of H&M.

For the designs, I how each screen and have written defined the flow and written notes about what each screen does and how it functions.

### General design considerations

Before I delve deeper into the design. Here are some general notes I made whilst writing the design.

**General Problems:**

- Text could be hard to read. Especially for colourblind people/people who have trouble distinguishing contrast in colours. Having a certain background behind the letters helps to increase readability and to divert attention to certain actions.
- Accessibility in case of height/disabilities need to be taken care of.
- Screen will dirty quick. How will this get handled? This could affect the placement of the UI.

**General Notes:**

- For this high fidelity design: Text with background has been chosen for increased readability and active diversion of attention.
- Darkened screen chosen for specific functionalities.

### Welcome Screen

This is what is showed when the shopper first enters the fitting room.

User can either choose to continue without logging in with their H&M account or receive more personalised recommendations when they do choose to register in it.

In case of “logging in” imagine a system where similar to the Burger King Kiosks which allows customers to interact with the kiosk temporarily using their account information. Imagine that being logged in to the mirror, it already knows important information about you. It knows your height, and your sizes, along with your purchase history. Based on this, it will be able to provide a more accurate shopping experience based on factual information rather than inferring everything momentarily.

### Home Screen

If we chose to sign in, we move onto the Home Screen.

This is the Home screen when the user is signed in.

In this screen There are multiple possible options provided as an example: The user may:

- Navigate back to the home screen.
- Check known clothes sizes and adjust if necessary.
- Start analysation process to provide fashion advice to the user.

The user is aided by the UI to properly navigate to the next logical step. Notice how the Start Scan button is more noticeable than the others.

### Sizes

The user is shown their current selected gender, height and known sizes for clothing pieces.

It is possible to adjust these sizes. Adjusting the sizes may affect the recommendations of clothing.

The clothing size determines what kind of clothes are available and what goes well together.

### Help

Provides some basic explanation of what the mirror provides in terms of functionality.

### Start Scanning

User has started the scanning process and has the ability to stop this. This is an intermediary screen shown before the analysation has succesfully occurred.

### Recommendations

This screens shows recommended clothing items to the user.

After the scanning has been completed which takes into account your current outfit and any known preferences. an array of outfits is established.

The outfits can be made of of different combinations. The user may select different fitting clothing items based on the other clothing items or change switch the a whole other outfit all together.

Shown above there is 1x pair of pants & 1x pair of shoes. But with this specific combination, multiple shirts may be chosen. And it gives an immediate look on how it can look based on how the pictures are laid out and composed within the screen.

### How are your recommendations made?

When pressing the How? button, a modal / Text is shown rather then navigation to a new screen occurring. Screen gets greyed out.

Why? So unnecessary navigation is ruled out not to confuse the user. And to continue the current flow of scanning without interruptions.

The user is shown information which informs them on the choices that are made to recommend them certain clothing.

## Group Feedback

Our team got together to provide each other with feedback regarding the designs and prototypes before we choose to present it to the client. The feedback me and Ikrame have received on the prototype is that the user flow could be more linear, inspired by the design of Jay and Dora.

This allows the user to have a more streamlined experience whilst simultaneously having less options which also cuts down development time. In general this is good feedback. 2 of the previously mentioned screens: How does it work & How are your recommendations made contain only static information. Therefore it’s been concluded that this could be displayed next to the screen like on a little board for example, which removes unnecessary navigation and readily provides useful information to the user at all times.
