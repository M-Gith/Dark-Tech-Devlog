# Smart mirror – From Concept to Physical Prototype

Following the initial design phase of our project, the second sprint was primarily focused on bringing our concept closer to reality. Whereas the first sprint was largely about exploring different possibilities for our smart mirror and establishing the direction of the project, this sprint was about taking the first steps towards actually realising it. With the Society 5.0 festival approaching, we had a concrete deadline to work towards. Our goal was to have a first iteration of our provocatype ready to present to visitors and receive feedback on.

## Starting the second sprint: Establishing our direction

After the client meeting at the end of the previous sprint, it had become clear that we would continue with the at-home mirror concept. With this direction established, we needed to start thinking about how we would actually realise the product. During the beginning of the second sprint, we started creating user stories and dividing the associated tasks amongst the team members.

I think this was a logical next step since it allowed us to break down the project into smaller and more manageable pieces. Rather than having a relatively abstract idea of what we wanted to make, we now had concrete tasks that we could start working on. This also allowed us to distribute responsibilities and give everyone a clearer idea of what they could contribute towards the development of the prototype.

However, as the sprint progressed, I noticed that we could sometimes lack a clear direction regarding what should take priority. This became particularly apparent when we were approximately 1.5 weeks away from the Society 5.0 festival. At that point, we still had quite a lot of work to do and I felt that we needed to reconsider our priorities.

An eager team member suggested that we simply start working through all the user stories we had established. While I appreciate the enthusiasm and the willingness to get things done, this approach did not entirely sit right with me. In my opinion, we first needed to establish what was actually necessary to have ready for Society 5.0. Completing every user story was not necessarily the most important thing at that moment. We needed to distinguish between the essential functionalities required to demonstrate our concept and the additional features that we could implement at a later stage.

I therefore posed the question of how we should proceed and suggested focusing more on the aspects that needed to be ready for the festival. I believe this was an important moment during the sprint because it helped bring attention back to what we were actually working towards. Having a clear deadline means that priorities sometimes have to change, and I think recognising this in time is an important part of working towards a successful result.

## Developing the first iteration

With our direction established, we started working towards the first physical iteration of the smart mirror.

The concept we are currently working towards consists of a double-sided mirror with a screen positioned behind it. This allows the user to see themselves in the mirror while simultaneously being presented with information and recommendations through the screen. The idea is to create an experience in which the user stands in front of the mirror and is presented with clothing recommendations based on their appearance.

For this iteration, the primary objective was not necessarily to have every aspect of the final product working perfectly. Instead, we wanted to create something that could already communicate the basic concept of the smart mirror. This would allow us to demonstrate our idea at Society 5.0 and, more importantly, receive feedback that could help us determine how to proceed with further development.

Something I particularly enjoyed about this development phase was seeing the physical prototype come together. During the first sprint, most of our work consisted of Figma designs and exploring different possible implementations. Although these designs gave us a good impression of what the product could look like, seeing the actual physical product made the project feel much more tangible.

I must admit that the physical prototype looked a lot better than I had initially expected. Seeing the screen integrated behind the mirror gave me a much clearer idea of what we were trying to accomplish and made me more enthusiastic about the direction of the project. It also made me realise how different it can be to evaluate an idea when you are actually able to see and experience it, rather than only looking at a design on a screen.

I think this is an important part of the design and realisation process. A design can look promising in theory, but certain aspects only become apparent when you start building something tangible. By creating an initial physical iteration, we have given ourselves something that we can further evaluate, improve and build upon.

## My contribution: Exploring the software side

When considering my personal contribution during this sprint, I initially wanted to focus more on the programming aspects of the project. In particular, I wanted to get involved with the backend development since I enjoy working with software and wanted to contribute more directly to the technical implementation.

For this, we made use of Vue.js for the frontend and Python for the backend, using a Spring-like Python framework called PySpring. Since I had primarily worked with other technologies before, this also presented an opportunity to familiarise myself with a different programming environment.

However, another team member had already made considerable progress on the backend implementation. This meant that there was less room for me to contribute directly to the existing development than I had initially anticipated. Combined with some personal commitments during this period, I found it somewhat difficult to get started and properly distribute my time between the project and my other responsibilities.

Nevertheless, I did spend time getting acquainted with the existing Python code and understanding how the initial backend structure had been established. This also meant familiarising myself with the installation process and the fundamentals of PySpring before I could realistically start contributing to the implementation itself.

Although I would have liked to have contributed more directly to the software, I think this experience has given me a clearer idea of where I want to focus my attention during the next sprint. I enjoy the technical side of software development and would like to take on a more substantial role in actually building the application. For the coming sprint, I therefore want to make sure that I plan my time better and establish earlier which technical tasks I can take responsibility for.

## Society 5.0: Presenting our first iteration

After working towards our initial prototype, we eventually had the opportunity to present it during the Society 5.0 festival.

This was an important moment for our team since it allowed us to show something tangible rather than simply explaining our concept through designs and presentations. We were also able to receive feedback from people outside our immediate project group, which gave us another perspective on how the concept was perceived.

During the feedback sessions, we received quite a bit of input regarding our prototype. This is particularly useful because, as developers, it can sometimes be difficult to distance ourselves from the ideas and assumptions we have established throughout the design process. Having other people interact with the product allows us to identify aspects that we might otherwise overlook.

We also had another meeting with our client. One of the points she brought forward was that she would have liked to see more provocative elements incorporated into the experience. This is an interesting point considering the original purpose of our project. We are not simply developing a smart mirror that recommends clothing. We want to demonstrate how technology can influence our preferences and potentially manipulate our purchasing decisions through repeated exposure to certain recommendations.

I think this is something we should explore further during the next sprint. If the prototype only demonstrates a convenient way of receiving clothing recommendations, we risk losing an important part of what makes this project interesting. The provocative aspect should be more apparent so that users are encouraged to question the recommendations they receive and reflect on how their preferences may be influenced.

At the time of writing this devlog, we still need to process and evaluate the feedback we received. I think it is important that we do not simply collect feedback for the sake of having it, but actually translate it into concrete improvements to our prototype. This is something we will have to take into account when determining our priorities for the next sprint.

## Teamwork and personal leadership

Besides the actual development of the prototype, I think another important aspect of this sprint was the way we operated as a team.

I generally try to be as active as possible within the group. I believe I have a fairly good understanding of the strengths of my team members and try to recognise when someone is particularly suited for a certain task or situation. I also try to step in when I notice that the team is lacking direction or when something needs to be addressed.

As previously mentioned, the situation regarding the Society 5.0 deadline was one example of this. I felt that it was important to question whether our current approach was actually helping us achieve our immediate goal. I do not necessarily see leadership as being the person who makes every decision or delegates every task. In my opinion, it is more about recognising when something needs to happen and helping the group move towards a solution without having to impose yourself on others.

Another aspect I consider important is maintaining a positive atmosphere within the team. Although this might seem like a relatively small contribution, I believe that having an enjoyable and comfortable working environment is incredibly important when developing something together. People should feel comfortable sharing ideas, making mistakes and discussing problems without having to worry about the atmosphere becoming hostile.

Unfortunately, during our last retrospective, some tensions arose between two team members. I must admit that I felt somewhat uncomfortable with the situation. I think disagreements are perfectly normal when working together, especially when people have different opinions and approaches. However, I also believe that it is important to ensure that these disagreements do not negatively affect the overall working environment.

I therefore tried to step in and emphasise that we should make an effort to keep the atmosphere positive and constructive. I do not think every disagreement needs to be avoided, but the way we communicate with each other matters. We should be able to address problems without allowing them to turn into personal conflicts.

Looking back, I think I have some strengths that allow me to contribute positively to the team in this regard. I consider myself relatively good at recognising what needs to be prioritised, reading people and communicating within a group. At the same time, I recognise that planning and organising my own time are areas where I still have room for improvement. This sprint has made that particularly apparent.

## What is next?

All in all, I think the second sprint has been a good step forward for our project. We now have a physical prototype that demonstrates the basic concept of our smart mirror, and we have received feedback that can help us determine how to improve it further.

However, there is still quite a lot of work to do. The current prototype is not yet a fully functional smart mirror. We still need to develop the software further and address some of the physical aspects before we can consider the product complete. More importantly, we need to explore how we can incorporate more provocative elements into the experience, as suggested by our client.

For the next sprint, I think our primary objective should be to establish a working product that we can continue building upon. Rather than having to establish the entire foundation from scratch during the final sprint, we should aim to have a functional iteration that allows us to add, remove and improve specific features.

Personally, I want to contribute more directly to the software development. I also want to improve my planning so that I can distribute my time more effectively and avoid finding myself in a situation where I have limited time to contribute to the actual implementation. I think this is something I can realistically improve by establishing clearer weekly priorities and being more proactive about taking ownership of technical tasks.

I am also satisfied with the role I have taken within the team. I think I have managed to contribute both socially and by helping steer the group in situations where direction was needed, without having to resort to forceful leadership. Nevertheless, I would like to find a better balance between these responsibilities and my own technical contributions.

All in all, we have made some good progress. We now have something tangible to work with, and I am looking forward to seeing how much further we can take the project during the next sprint!
